## 1. What is a nucleon?
A nucleon is either a proton or a neutron, the two particles that make up atomic nuclei. They are bound within the nucleus by the strong nuclear force.

## 2. Electromagnetic Coupling Constant

### a) Expression of the Dimensionless Electromagnetic Coupling Constant $\alpha_{EM}$ for Two Protons
The electromagnetic coupling constant (also known as the fine-structure constant) for two protons is defined as:
$$
\alpha_{EM} = \frac{e^2}{4 \pi \epsilon_0 \hbar c}
$$
### b) Typical Value of $\alpha_{EM}$
The typical value of $\alpha_{EM}$ is approximately:
$$
\alpha_{EM} \approx \frac{1}{137} \approx 0.0073
$$

## 3. Strong Interaction Potential

### a) Shape, Energy, and Length of Strong Interaction Potential
- **Shape**: The strong interaction potential is typically represented as a short-range attractive potential that rises steeply at small distances due to repulsive core effects.
- **Typical Energy**: ~10-100 MeV.
- **Typical Length Scale**: ~1 fm.

### b) Calculation of Dimensionless Strong Coupling Constant $\alpha_S$ for Two Protons at 1 fm
The strong coupling constant \( \alpha_S \) is calculated using the potential energy of the strong interaction \( V \) at 1 fm:
$$
\alpha_S = \frac{V \cdot r}{\hbar c}
$$
where $r = 1 \, \text{fm}$.
## 4. Spin of a Nucleon
### a) Value of Spin \( s \) of a Nucleon
The spin \( s \) of a nucleon is:
$$
s = \frac{1}{2}
$$
### b) Possible Values of Total Spin \( S \) for a System of Two Nucleons
The possible values of the total spin \( S \) for a system of two nucleons are:
$$
S = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad S = 1
$$
## 5. Isospin of a Nucleon
### a) Value of Isospin \( t \) of a Nucleon
The isospin \( t \) of a nucleon (either proton or neutron) is:
$$
t = \frac{1}{2}
$$
### b) Possible Values of Total Isospin \( T \) for a System of Two Nucleons
The possible values of the total isospin \( T \) for a system of two nucleons are:
$$
T = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad T = 1
$$
## 6. Generalized Pauli Principle
The generalized Pauli principle states that the total wavefunction of two fermions (e.g., nucleons) must be antisymmetric under exchange. This includes spatial, spin, and isospin components of the wavefunction.
## 7. Spin-Dependent Term of the Strong Interaction

### a) Expression of the Spin-Dependent Term
The spin-dependent term of the strong interaction between two nucleons, considering spins \( s_1 \) and \( s_2 \), is given by:
$$
V_{spin} \propto s_1 \cdot s_2
$$

### b) Dependence on Total Spin \( S \)
Using the identity $s_1 \cdot s_2 = \frac{1}{2}(S^2 - s_1^2 - s_2^2$), we find:
- For $S = 1:  s_1 \cdot s_2 = +\frac{1}{4}$ 
- For $S = 0: s_1 \cdot s_2 = -\frac{3}{4}$
## 8. Strong Interaction and Spin Dependence
From the results in questions 6 and 7, the strong interaction is more attractive in the $S = 1$ channel than in the $S = 0$ channel. This is because the $S = 1$ state aligns spins, increasing the attractive potential energy due to the spin-dependent term.
## 9. Binding of the Deuteron
For $L = 0$ states:
- The neutron-proton system (deuteron) is bound due to the attractive spin-dependent potential in the $S = 1$ state.
- The di-neutron and di-proton are unbound because, for same-nucleon pairs, the Pauli exclusion principle prevents pairing in the $S = 1$ state, leaving them in the less attractive $S = 0$ state.
## 10. Shell Model Configuration of Two Nucleons
For a system of two nucleons, the corresponding shell model configuration in terms of quantum numbers $nlj$ (principal quantum number $n$, orbital angular momentum $l$, total angular momentum $j$ is:
$$
n = 1, \quad l = 0, \quad j = \frac{1}{2}
$$
