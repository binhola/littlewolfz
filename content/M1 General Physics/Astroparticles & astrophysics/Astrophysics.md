## The Interstellar Medium

### History
- 1951, detection of 21 cm (Ewen & Purcell, Oort & Muller)
- 1968, first polyatomic molecule: NH$_3$ 
- 1970s - 90s: infrared and sub-mm astronomy (molecular gas and dust)

##### Definition: 
> "Anything that is not stars" (Osterbrock)

##### ISM
- Contents: gas (99 %), dust (1 %), $\vec{B}$, cosmic rays, photons
- Physical properties: $\rho, \, n, \, T, \, p_s, \, \vec{v}$ 
- Physical processes: heating and cooling
*Notes*: 
- The ISM is not a system in thermal equilibrium, can be other kinds of equilibriums
- Cooling is more efficient with richer chemical species (more channels of electron transitions)
- Big bang nucleosynthesis (~ 75 % H), present day ISM (~ 70 %, 28 %, 2 % metal) because many generations of stars
##### Regions, phases of the ISM
- Characterised by the state of hydrogen: phases are pure. transition regions are thin. 
- Phases also correspond to temperature and density states.
###### Phases
- HIM (Hot interstellar medium) = Coronal gas: $2 \times 10^7$ K
- WIM: warm ionised medium 
	- $H_{II}$ regions
- WNM: warm neutral medium
- CNM: cold neutral medium
###### The multi-phase interstellar gas
The various gas phases exist in pressure equilibrium:
$$
n \cdot T \approx 10^3 - 10^4 \, \rm K \, cm^{-3}
$$
Need the injection of energy flows through the system.

#### Heating and cooling
##### Heating
1. Photo-ionisation
2. Photo-dissociation
3. X-rays and cosmic rays
4. Photo-electrons (dust)
5. shocks form supernovaes
##### Cooling
- Conduction and convection (may play a role if enough timescale) negligible (low density of ISM)
- mainly through emitting radiations

Continuum
- Thermal emission
	- Black body
	- Dust
	- Mainly in far infrared and sub-mm for dust, UV, visible for stars
- Synchrotron emission
	- Acceleration of e in B
- Bresstrahlung or free-free emission

Lines
- Emissions lines
- Hyperfine structure

Coronal gas (HIM)
- very hot, tenuous gas
- filled half of the volume, 1 % mass, contains also very high ionized species, such as $O_{VI}$ 
- heated to $10^5 - 10^6$ K by the fast stellar winds and blast waves from novae and supernovaes.
