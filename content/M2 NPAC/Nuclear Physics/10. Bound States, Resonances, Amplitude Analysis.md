#### **Classification of Hadronic States**

Strongly interacting particles (hadrons) can be classified into three types based on their analytic structure in the S-matrix:

1. **Bound States**
   - **Location**: Poles on the **physical sheet** of the real $s$-axis **below threshold**
   - **Causality**: Must lie below the lowest production threshold
   - **Example**: Deuteron (proton-neutron bound state)

2. **Virtual States**  
   - **Location**: Poles on the **real $s$-axis** but on **unphysical Riemann sheets**
   - **Properties**: Close to threshold, enhance scattering lengths
   - **Example**: Isospin-1, spin-0 nucleon-nucleon scattering

3. **Resonances**
   - **Location**: Poles in the **complex plane** on **unphysical sheets**
   - **Properties**: Come in complex conjugate pairs $(s, s^*)$
   - **Width**: Finite imaginary part $\Gamma$ related to lifetime $\tau = 1/\Gamma$

#### **S-Matrix Analytic Structure**

The S-matrix is a unitary operator connecting asymptotic states and has key analytic features:

- **Branch Points**: Occur at kinematic thresholds
  - **Right-hand cuts**: Physical region thresholds (e.g., $\bar{K}K$ threshold in $\pi\pi$ scattering)
  - **Left-hand cuts**: Unphysical region from crossed channels

- **Poles**: Reveal the fundamental states of the theory
  - Physical sheet poles → Bound states
  - Unphysical sheet poles → Resonances and virtual states

#### **Experimental Classification**

In practice, we distinguish:
- **Bound States**: Stable under strong interaction (decay via weak/EM forces)
  - Examples: $B^+$, $D^0$ mesons, proton

- **Resonances**: Decay via strong interaction (finite width)
  - Examples: $J/\psi$, $\Lambda^+$ (despite different lifetimes)

#### **Resonance Signatures**
A true resonance exhibits:
1. **Peak** in cross section or invariant mass distribution
2. **Phase Motion**: Characteristic phase variation in the amplitude
3. **Quantum Interference**: With other intermediate states sharing the same initial/final states
#### **Partial Wave Analysis**
Due to rotational invariance, the S-matrix commutes with angular momentum operators:

- **Decomposition**: S-matrix splits into blocks for each $(J, P)$
  - $J$: Total angular momentum
  - $P$: Parity ($\pm 1$)

- **Method**: Expand in angular momentum eigenstates
- **Purpose**: Determine spin and parity of resonances
- **Application**: Essential for amplitude analysis in multi-body decays

#### **Key Points**
- **Dalitz Plots**: Study resonance structure in 3-body decays
- **Phase Space vs. Dynamics**: Uniform phase space vs. resonance peaks
- **Amplitude Analysis**: Extract quantum numbers from angular distributions

This framework provides the theoretical foundation for identifying and characterizing hadronic states through their analytic properties in scattering amplitudes.

### Substructure of Hadrons
Hadrons are composite particles made of **quarks** (spin-1/2 fermions) and **gluons** (spin-1 bosons). Their properties are determined by their constituent quarks.
#### Quark Masses: Higgs vs. Constituent Mass

Quarks have two different "masses" that reflect different physical origins:

| Quark | Higgs ("Bare") Mass | Constituent Mass (in Hadrons) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Up (u)** | ~2 MeV | ~350 MeV |
| **Down (d)** | ~5 MeV | ~350 MeV |
| **Strange (s)** | ~100 MeV | ~500 MeV |
| **Charm (c)** | ~1300 MeV | ~1500 MeV |
| **Bottom (b)** | ~4500 MeV | ~4700 MeV |
| **Top (t)** | ~173 GeV | ~173 GeV |
**Key Insight**: For light quarks (u, d, s), most of the hadron's mass comes from the **gluon field energy** (via $E=mc^2$), not from the Higgs mechanism. The "constituent mass" includes this binding energy.

#### Classification of Hadrons

| Type | Composition | Statistics | Baryon Number (B) | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mesons** | Quark + Antiquark $(q\bar{q})$ | Bosons (integer spin) | 0 | Pions $(u\bar{d})$, Kaons $(s\bar{u})$ |
| **Baryons** | Three Quarks $(qqq)$ | Fermions (half-integer spin) | +1 | Proton $(uud)$, Neutron $(udd)$ |
| **Antibaryons** | Three Antiquarks $(\bar{q}\bar{q}\bar{q})$ | Fermions | -1 | Antiproton $(\bar{u}\bar{u}\bar{d})$ |

#### Conventional vs. Exotic Hadrons

**Conventional Hadrons**
- Minimal color-singlet combinations allowed by QCD
- Mesons: $q\bar{q}$ | Baryons: $qqq$
- ~99% of known hadrons fall into these categories

**Exotic Hadrons** - Beyond the minimal quark content:

| Type | Composition | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Glueballs** | Gluons only $(gg, ggg)$ | Pure gluon bound states |
| **Tetraquarks** | $qq\bar{q}\bar{q}$ | Could be compact or molecular |
| **Pentaquarks** | $qqqq\bar{q}$ | Baryon-like with extra quark-antiquark pair |
| **Hexaquarks** | $qqqqqq$ or $qqq\bar{q}\bar{q}\bar{q}$ | Dibaryon states |
**Note**: The distinction can be fuzzy due to quantum mechanical mixing between states.

| Quantum Number | Up (u) | Down (d) | Charm (c) | Strange (s) | Top (t) | Bottom (b) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |

**Gell-Mann–Nishijima Formula**:
$$
Q = I_3 + \frac{B+S+C+B'+T}{2}
$$
- Up quark: $Q = I_3 + \frac{B+S+C+B'+T}{2} = +\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1/3}{2} = +\frac{2}{3}$ ✓
- Strange quark: $Q = 0 + \frac{1/3 + (-1)}{2} = -\frac{1}{3}$ ✓

#### Mesons: Quantum Numbers & Classification
**Angular Momentum**: $\vec{J} = \vec{l} + \vec{s}$ ($s = 0,1$; $l = 0,1,2...$)

**Quantum Number Formulas**:

| Quantity | Formula | Conditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Parity (P) | $P = -(-1)^l$ | Always |
| C-parity | $C = (-1)^{l+s}$ | Neutral, hidden flavour |
| G-parity | $G = (-1)^{I+l+s}$ | Hidden flavour |

**Light Meson Multiplets**:
- **Pseudoscalar** ($0^{-+}$): π⁺, π⁰, π⁻, K⁺, K⁰, K⁻, K̄⁰, η, η′
- **Vector** ($1^{--}$): ρ⁺, ρ⁰, ρ⁻, K*⁺, K*⁰, K*⁻, K̄*⁰, ω, φ

**Group Theory**: $3 \times \bar{3} = 1 + 8$

**Exotic Signals**: $0^{--}$, $0^{+-}$, $1^{-+}$, $2^{+-}$ forbidden for $q\bar{q}$
#### Baryons: Quantum Numbers & Classification

**Baryon Types**:

| Type | Quarks | Isospin | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| N | u,d only | I = 1/2 | p (uud), n (udd) |
| Δ | u,d only | I = 3/2 | Δ⁺⁺ (uuu) |
| Λ | uds | I = 0 | Λ⁰ |
| Σ | uus, uds, dds | I = 1 | Σ⁺, Σ⁰, Σ⁻ |
| Ξ | uss, dss | I = 1/2 | Ξ⁰, Ξ⁻ |
| Ω | sss | I = 0 | Ω⁻ |

**Parity**: 
- Baryon: $P = (-1)^{l_ρ}(-1)^{l_λ}$
- Antibaryon: $P = -(-1)^{l_ρ}(-1)^{l_λ}$

**SU(3) Structure**: $3 × 3 × 3 = 1 + 8 + 8 + 10$

**Ground States**: $\frac{1}{2}^+$ octet & $\frac{3}{2}^+$ decuplet

**Wave Function**: Color (antisymmetric) + Spin-flavour (symmetric)

#### Key Physical Insights

1. **Goldstone Bosons**: Massless pseudoscalars if quark masses = 0
2. **Symmetry Breaking**: SU(3) breaking explains mass splittings
3. **Exotic Signals**: Certain $J^{PC}$ indicate non-$q\bar{q}$ structure
4. **Mass Origin**: Most hadron mass from gluons, not Higgs
