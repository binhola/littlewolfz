### Deep inelastic scattering
#### Physical set up and kinematics
Scatter an $e^-$ off an proton. 
- Electron has 4-momentum $$k = (E, \vec{k}) \to k' = (E', \vec{k}')$$
- Proton has initial 4-momentum $p = (M, 0)$ and is broken up into undetected hadronic state $X$ with mass $W$ and four momentum $p'$.

The interaction is mediated by a virtual photon with 4-momentum:
$$
q = k - k'
$$
We define the key Lorentz-invariant kinematic variables:
1. **Virtuality:** $Q^2 = -q^2$ . This is positive for space-like photon. It sets the **resolution scale** of the probe. High $Q^2 \to$ short wavelength $\to$ higher resolution.
2. **Energy transfer (in proton rest frame):** $\nu = E' - E = \dfrac{p \cdot q}{M}$ . It's the energy lost by the electron.
3. **Bjorken:** $x = \dfrac{Q^2}{2 p \cdot q} = \dfrac{Q^2}{2 M \nu}$ . It's the fraction of proton's momentum carried by struck constituent.
4. **Invariant mass of hadronic final state**
$$
W^2 = (p + q)^2 = M^2 + 2M\nu - Q^2 
$$
For the process to be inelastic (i.e. to break the proton) , we require $W^2 > M^2$ , which leads to the kinematic limit:
$$
2 M\nu > Q^2
$$
In general, we have $0 \leq x \leq 1$
- $x=1$ is **elastic scattering** $W^2 = M^2$.
- $0 \leq x \leq 1$ is **inelastic scattering**

#### The cross section and the Hadronic tensor
The S-matrix element for $e + N \to e + X$:
$$
\langle e(k') X (p') | S | e(k) N(p) \rangle = -i (2\pi)^4 \delta^4 (p' - p - q) (4\pi \alpha) \bar u(k') \gamma_u u(k) \dfrac{1}{q^2} \langle X(p') | J^\mu(0) | N(p) \rangle
$$
Since we don't observe the final hadronic state of $X$, we must sum over all the possible states. This leads to the differential cross section as the product of a leptonic tensor (known from QED) and a hadronic tensor (unknown, containing proton structure):
$$
\dfrac{d^2\sigma}{dE' d\Omega'} = \dfrac{2 \alpha^2}{Q^4} \dfrac{E'}{E} L^{\mu \nu} W_{\mu \nu}
$$
- **Leptonic tensor:** $L^{\mu \nu} = k'^\nu k^\mu + k^\nu k'^\mu - g^{\mu \nu}(k \cdot k')$ , described the electron-photon vertex.
- **Hadronic tensor:** $W^{\mu \nu}$ described the photon-proton interaction and contain all the non-perturbative strong interaction physics.

The hadronic tensor is defined as:
$$
W_{\mu \nu}=\frac{1}{4 \pi M} \sum_X \int \frac{d^3 p^{\prime}}{(2 \pi)^3 2 E_X}(2 \pi)^4 \delta^4\left(p^{\prime}-p-q\right)\langle N(p)| J_\mu(0)\left|X\left(p^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle\left\langle X\left(p^{\prime}\right)\right| J_\nu(0)|N(p)\rangle
$$
where we sum over all possible hadronic final states $X$ and their spins.

#### **Parameterizing the Unknown: Structure Functions**

The hadronic tensor is not a completely arbitrary object. It must satisfy several constraints:
1. Depends only on $p$ and $q$
2. Symmetric: $W^{\mu \nu} = W^{\nu \mu}$
3. **Current Conservation:** $q^\mu W_{\mu \nu} = 0$

The most general tensor satisfying these constraints can be built from $g^{\mu \nu}, \, p_\mu$ and $q_\mu$ . Imposing the constraints, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mu \nu}= & \left(-g_{\mu \nu}+\frac{q_\mu q_\nu}{q^2}\right) W_1\left(\nu, Q^2\right)+\left(p_\mu-\frac{(p \cdot q) q_\mu}{q^2}\right)\left(p_\nu-\frac{(p \cdot q) q_\nu}{q^2}\right) \frac{1}{M^2} W_2\left(\nu, Q^2\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
The two unknown **Structure Functions**, $W_1(\nu, Q^2)$ and $W_2(\nu, Q^2)$, encapsulate all the information about the internal structure of the proton. Plugging this back into the cross-section formula and working in the lab frame gives the famous **Rosenbluth formula**:
$$
\frac{d^2 \sigma}{d E^{\prime} d \Omega^{\prime}}=\frac{4 \alpha^2 E^{\prime 2}}{Q^4}\left[2 W_1\left(\nu, Q^2\right) \sin ^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)+W_2\left(\nu, Q^2\right) \cos ^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right]
$$
This show that $W_1$ and $W_2$ separated by measuring the cross-section at different electron scattering angles $\theta$.
#### The Parton Model & The Breit frame
The Parton Model, proposed by Feynman, makes two key assumptions to interpret the structure functions:
1. The fast-moving nucleon is composed of point-like, quasi-free constituents called **partons** (which we now identify as quarks and gluons).
2. In high-energy collisions, the electron scatters **incoherently** off a single parton via a single photon exchange. The other partons are "spectators". 

A brilliant frame to visualize this is the **Breit Frame** (or "brick-wall" frame). In this frame, the virtual photon carries no energy, only 3-momentum. We align the z-axis with the photon's direction.

In this frame, the four-momenta are:
- Virtual photon: $q = (0,0,0,-Q)$
	- $q^2 = -Q^2$ space-like photon
	- has 3-momentum $Q$ but zero energy
- Fast proton: $p = (\sqrt{P^2 + M^2}, 0, 0, P) \approx (P, 0, 0, P)$ for $P >> M$ 

The parton carries a fraction $\xi$ of proton's momentum: $p_{\rm parton} = \xi p \sim (\xi P, 0, 0, \xi P)$ 

The scattering in this frame is beautifully simple. The photon "reflects" the parton, flipping its longitudinal momentum. Energy-momentum conservation at the parton-photon vertex gives:
$$
q + p_{\rm parton} = p_{\rm parton}'
$$
So
$$
(0,0,0,-Q) + (\xi P, 0, 0, \xi P) =  (\xi P, 0, 0, -\xi P)
$$
So
$$
\xi P = Q/2
$$
We have 
$$
2 M \nu = 2 p \cdot q = 2 P Q
$$
So 
$$
\xi = \dfrac{Q^2}{2PQ} = \dfrac{Q^2}{2M \nu} = x
$$
**Interpretation:** The kinematic variable $x$ is revealed as the **fraction of the proton's longitudinal momentum carried by the struck parton**.
#### **Bjorken Scaling and the Parton Model Calculation**
Bjorken predicted that in the **Bjorken Limit**:
$$
Q^2 \to \infty \quad \nu \to \infty \quad x = \dfrac{Q^2}{2 M\nu} \text{ fixed}
$$
the structure functions should exhibit **scaling**. That is, instead of depending independently on $\nu$ and $Q^2$, they become functions only of $x$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
2 M W_1(\nu, Q^2) &\to F_1(x) \\
\nu W_2(\nu, Q^2) &\to F_2(x)
\end{aligned}
$$
This was a revolutionary idea: no matter how high the resolution (how large $Q^2$), the proton appears to be made of the same point-like particles. The structure looks scale-invariant. 

The Parton Model provides a concrete calculation that realizes Bjorken scaling. The cross-section for DIS is simply the sum of cross-sections for elastic electron-parton scattering, weighted by the parton distribution functions (PDFs), $N_j(\xi)$, which give the number of parton type $j$ with momentum fraction between $\xi$ and $\xi + d\xi$. 

The result for spin-1/2 partons (quarks) is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& F_1(x)=\frac{1}{2} \sum_j Q_j^2 N_j(x) \\
& F_2(x)=x \sum_j Q_j^2 N_j(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

where $Q_j$ is the electric charge of parton $j$.
Interpretation:
- $F_2(x)$ is directly proportional to the momentum distribution of the charged constituents inside the proton.
- The factor of $x$ arises because the cross-section is sensitive to the parton's momentum fraction.
#### **Key Tests and Sum Rules**
#####  **The Callan-Gross Relation**
A critical test of the parton spin comes from the ratio $F_2 / F_1$. For spin-1/2 partons, the calculation gives:
$$
F_2(x) = 2x F_1(x)
$$
This is the **Callan-Gross relation**. Defining $R = \frac{F_2 - 2xF_1}{2xF_1}$, we find:
*   $R = 0$ for spin-1/2 partons.
*   $R \to \infty$ for spin-0 partons.
**Experimental data confirmed $R \approx 0$**, providing decisive evidence that the charged partons (quarks) have spin-1/2.

#### **Momentum and Quantum Number Sum Rules**
The Parton Model leads to fundamental sum rules that express conservation laws:
1.  **Momentum Sum Rule:** The total momentum of all partons must equal the proton's momentum.
	$$
    \int_0^1 dx \, x \left[ \sum_q (N_q(x) + N_{\bar{q}}(x)) + N_g(x) \right] = 1
    $$
    (Here $N_g(x)$ is the gluon distribution. Experiment shows quarks carry only ~50% of the proton's momentum, implying gluons carry the other half.)

2.  **Charge Sum Rule:** The sum of parton charges must equal the proton charge.
    $$
    \int_0^1 dx \sum_j Q_j N_j(x) = Q_p = 1
    $$
3.  **Baryon Number Sum Rule:** The net number of valence quarks (quarks minus antiquarks) must be 1.
$$
    \int_0^1 dx \, \frac{1}{3} \left[ N_u(x) - N_{\bar{u}}(x) + N_d(x) - N_{\bar{d}}(x) + ... \right] = 1
$$
#### **Summary and Interpretation**

| **Concept**         | **Mathematical Expression**             | **Physical Interpretation**                                   |
| :------------------ | :-------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Kinematics**      | $Q^2 = -q^2, \quad x=\frac{Q^2}{2M\nu}$ | $Q^2$ is the resolution; $x$ is the parton momentum fraction. |
| **Structure Fns**   | $W_{\mu\nu}$ from $W_1, W_2$            | Parameterize our ignorance of the proton's structure.         |
| **Bjorken Scaling** | $\nu W_2 \to F_2(x)$                    | Evidence of point-like, scale-invariant constituents.         |
| **Parton Model**    | $F_2(x) = x \sum_j Q_j^2 N_j(x)$        | $F_2(x)$ is the momentum distribution of quarks.              |
| **Callan-Gross**    | $F_2 = 2x F_1$                          | Proof that quarks have spin-1/2.                              |
| **Sum Rules**       | $\int x \sum N_j(x) dx = 1$             | Conservation of momentum, charge, and baryon number.          |

DIS and the parton model revealed the proton not as a fuzzy meson cloud, but as a vessel containing point-like, spin-1/2 quarks, whose momentum distributions could be directly measured, launching the era of quantitative QCD.

### Parton model in $e^+ e^-$ annihilation

The process is: $e^+ e^- \to \text{hadrons}$, via a virtual photon $\gamma^*$

**Idea:** At high energies, the virtual photon produces a quark-anti quark pair ($\gamma^* \to q \bar q$). These quarks hadronize, they dress themselves with other quarks and gluons from the vacuum to form the observed jets of hadrons. This is the reverse of DIS process.

**Observable:** The ratio $R$ is defined to compare the rate of hadron production to the well-know QED process of muon pair production:
$$
R(s) = \dfrac{\sigma(e^+ e^- \to \text{hadrons})}{\sigma(e^+ e^- \to \mu^+ \mu^-)}
$$
- The cross section for $e^+ e^- \to \mu^+ \mu^-$ is proportional to muon charge ($Q^2_\mu =1$)
- The cross section for $e^+ e^- \to q \bar q$ is proportional to the square of quark charge ($Q_q^2$)
- We must sum over all quark flavors $q$ that are kinematically allowed (i.e., where $\sqrt{s} > 2m_q$​)
- Crucially, if quarks come in $N_c$ colors, there are $N_c$ times many ways to produce $q \bar q$ than to a $\mu^+ \mu^-$.
This gives the famous result:
$$
R(s) = N_c \sum_q Q_q^2
$$
**Interpretation & Success:**
- For 3 colors ($N_c=3$) and three light quarks (u, d, s with charges $2/3$, $-1/3$, $-1/3$)
$$
R \approx 3 \times [(2/3)^2 + 2(-1/3)^2] = 2
$$
- As the energy increases and we can produce charm ($Q_c = 2/3$) and bottom ($Q_b = -1/3$), the value of $R$ jumps in steps: $2 \to 10/3 \to 11/3$.
- The data (Fig. 50) matches this prediction remarkably well, providing strong evidence for the existence of **three colors**.

#### **Parton Model in Hadron-Hadron Collisions: The Drell-Yan Process**
This is the "crossed" version of DIS. The process is:
$$
h_1 + h_2 \to \gamma^* \to l^+ l^- + X
$$
where a quark from one hadron and an antiquark from another hadron annihilate into a virtual photon, which then produces a lepton pair.

**The Idea:** It's like using the hadron collider as a "parton collider." The lepton pair doesn't interact strongly, so it cleanly probes the initial annihilation process.

**Kinematics:**
The invariant mass $m$ and rapidity $y$ of the lepton pair are directly related to the momentum fractions of the annihilating partons:
$$
x_{1,2} = \dfrac{m}{\sqrt s} e^{\pm y}
$$
- $x_1$ : Fraction of hadron 1's momentum carried by the quark.
- $x_2$ : Fraction of hadron 2's momentum carried by the anti-quark.
- **Interpretation:** By measuring $m$ and $y$, we can directly solve for $x_1$ and $x_2$, allow us to constraint the antiquark PDFs inside proton and nuclei, which are difficult to measure in DIS.

**Historical significance**: The key process for discover of $W$ and $Z$ bosons. A $W$ boson is produced in the same way ($q \bar q' \to W$) and its mass sets the scale $m$.

#### **The Master Formula for Hard Scattering (The Factorization Theorem)**
**QCD Factorization Theorem** is a general, powerful formula that underpins all modern calculations at colliders like the LHC.

For a hard process (like Drell-Yan, jet production, Higgs production) with a single hard scale $Q$, the cross-section can be factorized as:

$$
\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}(s, Q^2) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \alpha_S^n(\mu_R^2) \cdot \sum_{i,j} \int dx_1 dx_2\ N_{i/h_1}(x_i, \mu_F^2)\ N_{j/h_2}(x_j, \mu_F^2) \times \hat{\sigma}_{ij \rightarrow \mathcal{O}+X}(Q, \mu_R^2, \mu_F^2)
$$

Let's dissect this:

| **Term**                                      | **Physical Meaning**                                                                                                                                                                            |
| :-------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| $\alpha_S^n(\mu_R^2)$                         | **Perturbative Expansion:** The calculation is done order-by-order in the strong coupling constant $\alpha_S$. Higher orders (n=1,2,...) include loop diagrams and extra gluon radiation.       |
| $\sum_{i,j}$                                  | **Sum over Partons:** Sum over all possible initial parton pairs (e.g., $u\bar{u}$, $g g$, $u g$, etc.).                                                                                        |
| $\int dx_1 dx_2$                              | **Convolution over Momentum Fractions:** We integrate over all possible momentum fractions of the initial partons.                                                                              |
| $N_{i/h_1}(x_i, \mu_F^2)$                     | **Parton Distribution Functions (PDFs):** The **non-perturbative** probability to find a parton $i$ in hadron $h_1$ with momentum fraction $x_i$. These are the same functions measured in DIS. |
| $\hat{\sigma}_{ij \rightarrow \mathcal{O}+X}$ | **Partonic Cross-Section:** The **perturbative** (calculable) cross-section for the hard scattering of partons $i$ and $j$ to produce the desired final state $\mathcal{O}$.                    |

**The Key Scales $\mu_R$ and $\mu_F$:**
- **Renormalization Scale ($\mu_R$):** The scale at which we define the strength of the strong force ($\alpha_S$). It handles the ultraviolet divergences from loops.
- **Factorization Scale ($\mu_F$):** The scale that separates what we call "hard process" (in $\hat{\sigma}$) from what we call "parton distribution" (in PDFs). It absorbs the collinear divergences.

**Interpretation of Factorization:** This theorem is profound. It states that we can separate the calculation into two pieces:
1.  The **long-distance physics** (What's inside the proton? → encoded in universal PDFs).
2.  The **short-distance physics** (What happens when two partons point-like collide? → calculable in perturbative QCD).

This is why PDFs extracted from DIS at HERA can be used to predict cross-sections at the LHC.
### **4. The Emergence of Scale Dependence**
The text ends with a crucial point: the naive parton model had **Bjorken scaling** — the structure functions depended only on $x$. However, the full QCD formula introduces scale dependence ($\mu_F^2$).

**Why?** In QCD, a quark inside a proton can radiate a gluon. The probability of this happening depends on the resolution scale ($Q$) of the probe. The higher the $Q^2$, the more likely you are to see the quark resolved into a quark-plus-gluon system. This means the apparent number of low-momentum quarks changes with scale.

**Conclusion:** The PDFs are not static; they **evolve** with the scale $\mu_F^2$. This "violation" of scaling is a direct prediction of QCD and is described by the Dokshitzer-Gribov-Lipatov-Altarelli-Parisi (DGLAP) equations, which we will see in the next chapter. This is why the plot (Fig. 52) shows constraints in the 2D plane of $x$ vs. $Q^2$.
### Beyond the naive parton model: scaling violation in QCD
#### **The Core Idea: A Quantum Proton, Not a Classical One**
The naive parton model made a simplifying assumption: partons are non-interacting point particles moving collinearly with the proton. However, in QCD, the quarks and gluons are dynamic, interacting quantum fields.

**The New Picture: A Hierarchical Proton**
Think of the proton not as a bag of static particles, but as a constantly fluctuating cloud of quantum fields. What we call a "parton" depends on the **resolution scale (Q)** of our microscope.
- Low resolution (low $Q^2$): You see a few "effective" constituents—the three valence quarks and a few gluons and sea quarks.
- High resolution (high $Q^2$): You can see _inside_ these constituents. A quark can reveal itself as a quark plus a gluon it just radiated. A gluon can split into a quark-antiquark pair. You see a much denser and more complex soup of partons.

This is why the parton distribution functions (PDFs) must depend on the scale: $N_i(x,Q^2)$. The structure function scaling is "violated" because the underlying object changes as you probe it more finely.
$$
\nu W_2(\nu, Q^2) = x \sum_i Q_i^2 N_i(x, Q^2)
$$
**Interpretation:** Bjorken scaling is an approximation that holds when the energy scale is high enough to see point-like particles, but not so high that you see their quantum fluctuations. Full QCD predicts a gentle, calculable "violation" of this scaling.
#### **The Engine of Evolution: The DGLAP Equations**
The change of the parton densities with the scale $Q^2$ is precisely predicted by the **Dokshitzer-Gribov-Lipatov-Altarelli-Parisi (DGLAP)** equations.

**The Intuition:**  
As you increase the resolution $Q^2$, a parton carrying momentum fraction $x$ could actually have come from a **parent parton** with a _larger_ momentum fraction $x/z$ (where $z<1$) that radiated a gluon (or another quark), losing some of its momentum.

The DGLAP equation is a differential equation that describes this flow of momentum between partons as the scale changes.

**The Equation (Leading Order):**
$$
\frac{dN_i(x, \mu_F^2)}{d \log \mu_F^2} = \sum_j \int_x^1 \frac{dz}{z} \frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi} P_{i \leftarrow j}(z) N_j\left(\frac{x}{z}, \mu_F^2\right) = \frac{\alpha_S}{2\pi} \sum_j (P_{i \leftarrow j} \otimes N_j)(x, \mu_F^2)
$$
Let's dissect the components:

| **Term**                      | **Physical Meaning**                                                                                                                   |
| :---------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| $\frac{dN_i}{d \log \mu_F^2}$ | How the density of parton type $i$ at momentum fraction $x$ changes when we change the resolution scale.                               |
| $P_{i \leftarrow j}(z)$       | **Splitting Function.** The probability that parton $j$ will radiate and become parton $i$, carrying a fraction $z$ of $j$'s momentum. |
| $N_j(\frac{x}{z}, \mu_F^2)$   | The density of the *parent* parton $j$ at a higher momentum fraction $x/z$.                                                            |
| $\otimes$                     | **Convolution.** This integral sums over all possible momentum fractions $z$ of the splitting and all possible parent partons $j$.     |

**Key Splitting Processes:**
*   $P_{q \leftarrow g}(z)$: A gluon splits into a quark-antiquark pair. This increases the sea quark density.
*   $P_{g \leftarrow q}(z)$: A quark radiates a gluon. This increases the gluon density.
*   $P_{g \leftarrow g}(z)$: A gluon splits into two gluons. This leads to a rapid growth of gluons.
#### Consequences: The Modern Picture of the Proton**
The DGLAP evolution leads to a precise and testable picture of the proton's structure, confirmed by global data fits (like those from NNPDF, CTEQ, or MSHT).

**The Proton at Different Scales:**
1.  **High $x$ ( $x > 0.1$ ):** Dominated by the **valence quarks** (uud). These are the quarks that give the proton its quantum numbers.
2.  **Low $x$ ( $x < 0.01$ ):** Dominated by the **gluon and sea quark** densities.
    *   The gluon density **rises very steeply** as $x$ decreases.
    *   **Why?** The $g \rightarrow gg$ splitting function is very large at small $z$, meaning gluons are very likely to radiate other low-momentum gluons. This creates an intense, dense "gluon cloud" at low $x$.
3.  **Scale Evolution ($Q^2$):**
    *   As $Q^2$ increases, the densities at low $x$ are **fed** by the radiation from partons at higher  $x$.
    *   For example, a high-$x$ valence quark radiating a gluon will deplete the quark density at high $x$ and enhance the gluon density at a lower $x$.
#### **Summary Table: Naive Model vs. QCD Reality**

| Feature              | Naive Parton Model                  | QCD Picture (with DGLAP)                                                             |
| :------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Parton Nature**    | Static, point-like, non-interacting | Dynamic, fluctuating quantum fields                                                  |
| **PDF Dependence**   | $N_i(x)$                            | $N_i(x, Q^2)$                                                                        |
| **Scaling**          | Exact Bjorken Scaling               | Scaling Violations                                                                   |
| **Gluons**           | Ignored or passive                  | **Active drivers of evolution;** dominate at low $x$                             |
| **Prediction Power** | Qualitative                         | **Quantitative and Precise** (using NNLO calculations)                               |
| **Proton Picture**   | A bag of three quarks               | A dense, evolving core of valence quarks surrounded by a seething, low-$x$ gluon sea |

In conclusion, moving beyond the naive parton model reveals a much richer and more dynamic proton. The "scaling violations" are not a failure of the model but the signature of the quantum field theory of the strong force at work, allowing for precise and testable predictions that form the bedrock of all calculations at the LHC.

### Past, current and future DIS facilities: HERA, JLAB, Compass and EIC

#### **The Past: HERA – The Electron-Proton Microscope**
**HERA (Hadron-Electron Ring Accelerator) at DESY (Germany)** was the world's only **electron-proton collider**.
*   **Concept:** Collide 27.5 GeV electrons with 920 GeV protons, achieving a center-of-mass energy of 318 GeV. This was unique because it provided a "clean" probe (the electron) to explore a "dirty" target (the proton) at very high energies.
*   **Key Achievement:** HERA provided the **definitive, high-precision map of the proton's interior** over a huge range in $x$ and $Q^2$.
* **Legacy Result (Fig. 58):** The plot of the structure function $F_2(x, Q^2)$ is HERA's masterpiece. It beautifully demonstrates:
    *   **Bjorken Scaling (approx.):** For a fixed $x$, $F_2$ is roughly constant over a wide range of $Q^2$ (the points lie on vertical lines).
    *   **Scaling Violations (precisely):** The small but definite $Q^2$-dependence at each $x$ is perfectly described by the **DGLAP equations** of QCD. The data validates QCD evolution over an "impressive lever arm."
    *   HERA data became the **cornerstone** for all modern global PDF fits.

#### **3. The Future: The Electron-Ion Collider (EIC)**
There is a major gap in our understanding, especially regarding **nuclei** and the **fundamental origin of nucleon properties**. The EIC is designed to address this.

**The Big Unanswered Questions:**
1.  **The Nuclear Mystery:** When placed inside a nucleus, the parton distributions are **not simply the sum of free proton and neutron PDFs**. The ratio of nuclear to free nucleon PDFs shows unexpected structure (the "EMC effect," Fig. 59). We don't fully understand:
    *   Does the DGLAP evolution work for nuclei?
    *   What is the connection between this observed structure and traditional nuclear physics?

2.  **The Proton's Origin:**
    *   **Mass:** Where does the proton's mass come from? (Hint: The sum of the quark masses is only ~1% of the proton's mass; the rest comes from the dynamics of gluons and quark interactions via QCD).
    *   **Spin:** How is the proton's spin of $\frac{1}{2}$ built from the spins and orbital motions of its quarks and gluons?

3.  **The Density Frontier:** At very high energies (very low $x$), the gluon density inside a nucleon or nucleus is predicted to become so immense that the gluons overlap and form a novel, dense state of matter. Does the hadron remain a collection of dilute, independent partons, or does it become a **dense, interacting gluonic wall**?

**The EIC's Mission:**
The EIC will be a precision **electron-nucleus collider**. Its design will allow it to:
*   Systematically map the **3D structure** of quarks and gluons in protons and nuclei.
*   Precisely image the **gluon distributions** inside nuclei to solve the nuclear PDF mystery.
*   "Weigh" the contributions to the proton's **mass and spin** from gluons and quark orbital motion.
*   Search for the onset of the **high-density gluonic matter**.

In essence, while HERA told us *what* is inside the proton, the EIC will tell us *how* and *why* it gets there, uncovering the deeper rules of QCD.
