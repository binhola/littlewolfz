### **The Core Idea: Penetrating Probes**

**Hadrons** (like pions, protons) are created at the end of the collision ("freeze-out") and interact strongly, so they only tell us about the final state.

**Photons and Dileptons** are different:
*   They are produced via the electromagnetic force throughout the entire collision.
*   Once created, they escape the hot, dense QGP **without further interaction** because they don't feel the strong force.
*   This makes them ideal "messengers" that carry direct, unmodified information about the conditions inside the QGP at the moment they were created.

---

### **1. Direct Photons: A Thermometer for the QGP**

Photons are emitted from every stage of the collision. The key is to isolate the "thermal" photons that come from the QGP itself.

#### **What They Measure: Temperature**

The production rate of thermal photons from the QGP is proportional to the temperature:
$$
E_{\gamma} \frac{dN_{\gamma}}{d^3p} \propto e^{-E_{\gamma}/T}
$$
This looks like a thermal spectrum, acting as a **thermometer** for the fireball.

#### **The Complication: "Blue-Shift" from Flow**

The QGP is not static; it's a rapidly expanding fluid. The photons get a "kick" from this collective motion, which blueshifts their energy. What we measure is not the true temperature $T$, but an **effective temperature**:
$$
T_{\text{eff}} = \sqrt{\frac{1 + \beta_{\text{flow}}}{1 - \beta_{\text{flow}}}} \cdot T
$$
where $\beta_{\text{flow}}$ is the flow velocity.

*   **Key Insight:** This blueshift is largest at late times when the true temperature is low (~150-200 MeV). Therefore, the low-energy photon spectrum is dominated by the late, flowing stages. To extract the initial, hot temperature, we must compare data to sophisticated **hydrodynamic models** that account for this entire evolution.

#### **Experimental Finding:**
Experiments at RHIC and LHC observe an excess of direct photons over expectations from hadron decays. The extracted effective temperatures are very high (~300 MeV at the LHC), providing direct evidence for the creation of an extremely hot, thermalized medium.

### **2. Dileptons: A Tomographic Movie of the Collision**

Dileptons ($e^+e^-$ or $\mu^+\mu^-$ pairs) are even more powerful than single photons because their **invariant mass ($M$)** provides a powerful sorting tool.

#### **Low-Mass Region ($M < 1.5$ GeV): Probing Chiral Symmetry Restoration**

*   **The Physics:** In vacuum, the $\rho$ meson is a broad resonance. In the hot, dense QGP, chiral symmetry is expected to be restored, which should dramatically alter the properties of the $\rho$ meson (e.g., it might melt or broaden significantly).
*   **The Measurement:** The NA60 experiment observed a clear **broadening and mass shift** of the $\rho$ meson peak in the dilepton spectrum. This is considered the **most direct experimental evidence for chiral symmetry restoration** in heavy-ion collisions.

#### **Intermediate-Mass Region ($M > 1.5$ GeV): Probing the Earliest, Hottest Phase**

This is the most direct window into the initial conditions and thermalization of the QGP.

*   **Why it's sensitive to the early stage:** To create a high-mass dilepton pair, you need a lot of energy. At early times, the temperature is highest, making it the dominant source of these pairs.
*   **The Idealized Picture (Bjorken Expansion):** In a perfectly expanding fireball, the dilepton yield integrated over the entire history has a simple power-law form:
    $$
    \frac{dN_{ll}}{dM} \propto \frac{1}{M^3} \left( \frac{dN_{ch}}{d\eta} \right)^{4/3}
    $$
    Notice that the **temperature has canceled out**. The yield is directly sensitive to the **initial entropy density** (related to the charged particle multiplicity $dN_{ch}/d\eta$).

*   **The Real World: Sensitivity to Thermalization**
    The idealized picture assumes instant thermal equilibrium. In reality:
    1.  **Quark Suppression:** The initial state is gluon-dominated; it takes time for quarks to reach their equilibrium abundance.
    2.  **Pressure Anisotropy:** The early system is highly anisotropic; it takes time for it to isotropize and become a perfect fluid. The timescale for this is controlled by the **viscosity-to-entropy ratio ($\eta/s$)**.

    **Conclusion:** By measuring intermediate-mass dileptons and comparing to models, we can directly constrain:
    *   The **thermalization time** of the QGP.
    *   The **viscosity ($\eta/s$)** at the earliest times.

    This is a **unique and crucial measurement**, as this early-time dynamics is otherwise almost completely inaccessible to experiment.

### **Summary Table**

| Probe | What it Measures | Key Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Direct Photons** | **Temperature** of the QGP throughout its evolution. | The spectrum is blueshifted by flow, requiring complex modeling to extract the initial, high temperature. |
| **Low-Mass Dileptons** | **In-medium modification of hadrons** (e.g., the $\rho$ meson). | Provides the most direct evidence for **chiral symmetry restoration**. |
| **Intermediate-Mass Dileptons** | **Early-time conditions and thermalization.** | Sensitive to **quark suppression** and the **viscosity ($\eta/s$)** at the very beginning of the collision. |

In short, photons and dileptons act as a **combined thermometer and tomographic scanner** for the QGP, allowing us to probe its temperature, its internal symmetry restoration, and the fundamental process of how it thermalizes into a perfect liquid.
