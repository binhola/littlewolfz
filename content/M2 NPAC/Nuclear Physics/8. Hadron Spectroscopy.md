### Introduction to Hadron Spectroscopy
The study of *bound states* and *resonances* made of quarks and gluons.
**Key historical example:** the 1974 discovery of $J/\psi = c \bar c$particle revealed:
- The charm quark
- Confirmed QCD is the correct theory of strong interactions.

**Modern revival:** Since 2000s, experiments like **B-factories (Belle/BaBar)** and **LHCb** has discovered many new particles that don't fit traditional quark patterns $\to$renewing interest for this field.
### Basis definitions & Classifications
##### What is a hadron?
A **hadron** is any strongly interacting particle composed of quarks and gluons. 
- **Meson**: quark-antiquark pairs $q \bar q$
- **Bayrons:** three-quark states $qqq$
##### Production mechanism
Hadrons can be produced through:
1. Formation: 
$$
A + B \to R \to \text{ (decay product)}
$$
2. Associated Production:
$$
A + B \to R + S
$$
3. Decay:
$$
Z \to R + S
$$
##### Kinematics: Mandelstam variables
For a $2 \to 2$process $A+B \to C +D$:
$$
\begin{align*}
s &= (p_A + p_B)^2 \\
t &= (p_A - p_C)^2 \\
u &= (p_A - p_D)^2
\end{align*}
$$
So
$$
s + t + u = m_A^2 + m_B^2 + m_C^2 + m_D^2
$$
**Proof:**
$$
\begin{align*}
s &= (p_A + p_B)^2 = p_A^2 + p_B^2 + 2p_A\cdot p_B \\
t &= (p_A - p_C)^2 = p_A^2 + p_C^2 - 2p_A\cdot p_C \\
u &= (p_A - p_D)^2 = p_A^2 + p_D^2 - 2p_A\cdot p_D
\end{align*}
$$
Summing
$$
s + t + u = 3p_A^2 + p_B^2 + p_C^2 + p_D^2 + 2p_A\cdot(p_B - p_C - p_D)
$$
Using 
$$
p_A + p_B = p_C + p_D
$$
We have
$$
s + t + u = 3p_A^2 + p_B^2 + p_C^2 + p_D^2 + 2p_A\cdot(-p_A) = p_A^2 + p_B^2 + p_C^2 + p_D^2 = m_A^2 + m_B^2 + m_C^2 + m_D^2 \quad \square
$$
### Cross Section Derivation: From Quantum Mechanics to Relativistic Formula

#### **1. Quantum Mechanical Definition of Cross Section**

In non-relativistic quantum mechanics, the differential cross section is defined as:

$$
\dfrac{d \sigma}{d\Omega} = \dfrac{1}{F} \dfrac{d \mathcal{P}}{d\Omega}
$$

where the **flux factor** $F$ accounts for the relative velocity of incoming particles:

$$
F = \dfrac{|\vec{v}_a - \vec{v}_b|}{V}
$$

with relativistic velocity $\vec{v} = \dfrac{\vec{p}}{E}$.

**In the center of mass frame**: $\vec{p}_a = -\vec{p}_b = \vec{p}_i$ and $E_{CM} = E_a + E_b$, the flux becomes:

$$
F = \dfrac{|\vec{p}_i|}{V} \dfrac{E_{CM}}{E_a E_b}
$$

---

#### **2. Quantum Mechanical Transition Probability**

The differential probability for scattering into momentum range $d^3 p_k$ is:

$$
d \mathcal{P} = \dfrac{|\bra{f}S\ket{i}|^2}{\langle f | f \rangle \langle i | i \rangle} \prod_{k = 1}^{n} \left( \dfrac{V}{(2\pi)^3} d^3 p_k \right)
$$

The normalization ensures:

$$
\prod_{k = 1}^{n} \left( \dfrac{V}{(2\pi)^3} \int_{\mathbb{R}} d^3 p_k \right) = 1
$$

---

#### **3. State Normalization in Finite Volume**

**Initial state normalization**:

$$
\langle i | i \rangle = \langle p_a | p_a \rangle \langle p_b | p_b \rangle  = (2 E_a (2 \pi)^3 \delta^3(0)) (2 E_b (2 \pi)^3 \delta^3(0)) = (2 E_a V) (2 E_b V)
$$

using the representation:

$$
\delta^3(p) = \dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^3} \int_{\mathbb{R}} d^3 x e^{i \vec{p} \cdot \vec{x}} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \delta^3(0) = \dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^3} \int_{\mathbb{R}} d^3 x = \dfrac{V}{(2\pi)^3}
$$

**Final state normalization**:

$$
\langle f | f \rangle = \prod_{k=1}^n (2 E_k V)
$$

---

#### **4. S-Matrix and Transition Amplitude**

Decompose $S = 1 + iT$, where the non-trivial scattering is:

$$
\bra{f}S - 1\ket{i} = i\bra{f}T\ket{i} = i (2\pi)^4 \underbrace{\delta^4(p_a + p_b - \sum_{k=1}^n p_k)}_{\text{kinematics}} \, \underbrace{\bra{f} \mathcal{M}\ket{i}}_{\text{dynamics}}
$$

The **kinematic term** enforces energy-momentum conservation, while $\mathcal{M}$ contains the interaction dynamics.

---

#### **5. Squared Amplitude and Regularization**

$$
|\bra{f}T\ket{i}|^2 = (2\pi)^8 \delta^4(p_a + p_b - \sum_{k=1}^n p_k) \delta^4(0) \, | \bra{f} \mathcal{M}\ket{i} |^2
$$

Regularizing in finite volume $V$ and time $T$:

$$
\delta^4(0) = \dfrac{VT}{(2\pi)^4} \quad \Rightarrow \quad |\bra{f}T\ket{i}|^2 = (2\pi)^4 VT \, \delta^4(p_a + p_b - \sum_{k=1}^n p_k) \, | \bra{f} \mathcal{M}\ket{i} |^2
$$

---

#### **6. Cross Section Formula**

Since cross section is rate per unit flux:

$$
d\sigma = \dfrac{1}{TF} d \mathcal{P}
$$

Substituting all terms:

$$
d\sigma = \dfrac{1}{T} \dfrac{V}{|\vec{v}_a - \vec{v}_b|} \dfrac{(2\pi)^4 \, VT \, | \bra{f} \mathcal{M}\ket{i} |^2}{(2 E_a V)(2 E_b V)} \delta^4(p_a + p_b - \sum_{k=1}^n p_k) \prod_{k=1}^n \left[ \dfrac{1}{2 E_k V} \dfrac{V d^3 p_k}{(2\pi)^3} \right]
$$

Cancelling factors of $V$ and $T$:

$$
d\sigma = \dfrac{1}{|\vec{v}_a - \vec{v}_b|} \dfrac{(2\pi)^4 \, | \bra{f} \mathcal{M}\ket{i} |^2}{(2 E_a)(2 E_b)} \delta^4(p_a + p_b - \sum_{k=1}^n p_k) \prod_{k=1}^n \left[ \dfrac{1}{2 E_k} \dfrac{d^3 p_k}{(2\pi)^3} \right]
$$

---

#### **7. Lorentz Invariant Flux Factor**

Define the invariant flux:

$$
\mathcal{F} = (2E_a)(2 E_b) |\vec{v}_a - \vec{v}_b| = 4 \sqrt{(p_a \cdot p_b)^2 - m_a^2 m_b^2}
$$

**In center of mass frame**:

$$
\mathcal{F} = 4 \, p_i \, E_{CM}
$$

Thus, the master formula becomes:

$$
d\sigma = \dfrac{1}{\mathcal{F}} | \bra{f} \mathcal{M}\ket{i} |^2 d \mathrm{PS}^{n}
$$

where the **Lorentz Invariant Phase Space** is:

$$
d \mathrm{PS}^n = (2\pi)^4 \delta^4 (p_a + p_b - \sum_{k=1}^n p_k) \prod_{k=1}^n \left[ \dfrac{1}{2 E_k} \dfrac{d^3 p_k}{(2\pi)^3} \right] 
$$

---

#### **8. Two-Body Phase Space Evaluation**

For $2 \to 2$ process:

$$
d\mathrm{PS}^2 = (2\pi)^4 \delta^4 (p_a + p_b - p_1 - p_2) \dfrac{1}{2 E_1} \dfrac{1}{2 E_2} \dfrac{d^3 p_1}{(2\pi)^3} \dfrac{d^3 p_2}{(2\pi)^3}
$$

Integrate over $d^3 p_2$ using momentum conservation $\vec{p}_2 = -\vec{p}_1$:

$$
d\mathrm{PS}^2 = (2\pi) \delta (E_a + E_b - E_1 - E_2) \dfrac{1}{2 E_1} \dfrac{1}{2 E_2} \dfrac{d^3 p_1}{(2\pi)^3}
$$

Switch to spherical coordinates $d^3p_1 = p_f^2 dp_f d\Omega$:

$$
d\mathrm{PS}^2 = \dfrac{1}{16 \pi^2} d\Omega \, dp_f \dfrac{p_f^2}{E_1 E_2}  \delta (E_a + E_b - E_1 - E_2)
$$

---

#### **9. Delta Function Manipulation**

Let $f(p_f) = E_{CM} - E_1(p_f) - E_2(p_f)$, with $E_1 = \sqrt{m_1^2 + p_f^2}$, $E_2 = \sqrt{m_2^2 + p_f^2}$.

Using the **delta function scaling theorem**:

$$
\delta(f(x)) = \sum_i \dfrac{\delta(x-x_i)}{|f'(x_i)|}
$$

Compute derivative:

$$
\dfrac{dE_1}{dp_f} = \dfrac{p_f}{E_1}, \quad \dfrac{dE_2}{dp_f} = \dfrac{p_f}{E_2}
$$
$$
f'(p_f) = - \dfrac{p_f}{E_1} - \dfrac{p_f}{E_2} = -p_f \dfrac{E_{CM}}{E_1 E_2}
$$

Thus:

$$
\delta(E_{CM} - E_1 - E_2) = \dfrac{E_1 E_2}{p_f E_{CM}} \delta(p_f - p_f^*) \Theta(E_{CM} - m_1 - m_2)
$$

---

#### **10. Final Phase Space Expression**

Substitute and integrate:

$$
d\mathrm{PS}^2 = \dfrac{1}{16 \pi^2} d\Omega \, \dfrac{p_f}{E_{CM}} \Theta(E_{CM} - m_1 - m_2)
$$

---

#### **11. Final Differential Cross Section**

$$
d\sigma = \dfrac{1}{\mathcal{F}} | \mathcal{M} |^2 d \mathrm{PS}^{2} = \dfrac{1}{4 p_i E_{CM}} | \mathcal{M} |^2 \dfrac{1}{16 \pi^2} d\Omega \, \dfrac{p_f}{E_{CM}} \Theta(E_{CM} - m_1 - m_2)
$$

$$
\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \dfrac{1}{64 \pi^2 E_{CM}^2} \dfrac{p_f}{p_i} | \mathcal{M} |^2 \Theta(E_{CM} - m_1 - m_2)
$$
We can also use 
$$
E_{CM}^2 = s
$$
**Proof**:
$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= (p_a + p_b)^2 = p_a^2 + 2 p_a \cdot p_b + p_b^2 = m_a^2 + m_b^2 + 2 (E_a E_b - \vec{p_a} \cdot \vec{p_b}) \\
&= E_a^2 - p_i^2 + E_b^2 - p_i^2 + 2 E_a E_b + 2 p_i^2 \\
&= (E_a + E_b)^2 \\
&= E_{CM}^2 \quad \square
\end{aligned}
$$

---
#### **12. Example: Electron-Proton Scattering**

For $e^- p \to e^- p$ with $m_p \gg m_e$:

- $E_{CM} \approx m_p$ (proton mass dominates)
- Elastic scattering: $p_i = p_f$
- Heaviside function $\Theta = 1$ (kinematically allowed)

$$
\left(\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\right)_{CM} = \dfrac{1}{64 \pi^2 m_p^2} | \mathcal{M} |^2
$$

This matches the standard result for heavy target scattering, demonstrating the consistency of the derivation.
