#### **1. Decay Width and Lifetime**

For an unstable particle, the **decay width** $\Gamma$ and **lifetime** $\tau$ are related by:

$$
\tau = \frac{1}{\Gamma}, \quad \Gamma = \sum_i \Gamma_i
$$

where $\Gamma_i$ represents the partial decay width for each decay channel.

---

#### **2. Differential Decay Width**

Analogous to the cross section calculation, the **differential decay width** for a process $A \to 1 + 2 + \dots + n$ is derived from the transition probability.

From our S-matrix formalism:

$$
\bra{f}T\ket{i} = (2\pi)^4 \delta^4(p_A - \sum_{k=1}^n p_k) \mathcal{M}_{fi}
$$

The differential decay rate is:

$$
d\Gamma = \frac{1}{2E_A} |\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2 d\mathrm{PS}^n
$$

where the **Lorentz Invariant Phase Space** is:

$$
d\mathrm{PS}^n = (2\pi)^4 \delta^4(p_A - \sum_{k=1}^n p_k) \prod_{k=1}^n \frac{d^3p_k}{(2\pi)^3 2E_k}
$$

**Key differences from scattering**:
- Only **one initial particle** (the decaying particle)
- Energy-momentum conservation: $\delta^4(p_A - \sum p_k)$
- Normalization factor $\frac{1}{2E_A}$ comes from $\langle A|A \rangle = 2E_A V$

---

#### **3. Two-Body Decay**

For $A \to 1 + 2$, we start with:

$$
d\Gamma = \frac{1}{2E_A} |\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2 d\mathrm{PS}^2
$$

**In the rest frame of particle A**:
- $p_A = (m_A, \vec{0})$
- $E_A = m_A$
- Momentum conservation: $\vec{p}_1 = -\vec{p}_2$
- Let $|\vec{p}_1| = |\vec{p}_2| = p_f$

Using our previous phase space result for 2-body final states:

$$
d\mathrm{PS}^2 = \frac{1}{16\pi^2} \frac{p_f}{m_A} d\Omega
$$

Substituting:

$$
d\Gamma = \frac{1}{2m_A} |\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2 \cdot \frac{1}{16\pi^2} \frac{p_f}{m_A} d\Omega = \frac{p_f}{32\pi^2 m_A^2} |\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2 d\Omega
$$

Thus:

$$
d\Gamma(A \to 1 + 2) = \frac{p_f}{32\pi^2 m_A^2} |\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2 d\Omega_f
$$

where:
- $p_f$ is the magnitude of the 3-momentum in the rest frame
- $d\Omega_f$ is the solid angle element for one final particle

---

#### **4. Three-Body Decays and Dalitz Plots**

For three-body decays $A \to 1 + 2 + 3$, we use invariant mass variables:

Define:
$$
m_{12}^2 = (p_1 + p_2)^2, \quad m_{23}^2 = (p_2 + p_3)^2
$$

After integrating over the phase space, we obtain:

$$
d\Gamma(m_{12}^2, m_{23}^2) = \frac{1}{256\pi^3} \frac{1}{m_A^3} |\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2 dm_{12}^2 dm_{23}^2
$$

**This assumes**:
- Averaging over spins (or scalar particles)
- The matrix element $|\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2$ may depend on $m_{12}^2$ and $m_{23}^2$

---

#### **5. Dalitz Analysis**

The $(m_{12}^2, m_{23}^2)$ plane is called the **Dalitz plane**. Experimentally:

1. Measure event density in the Dalitz plane
2. Analyze distribution to extract:
   - **Resonances** (appearing as bands)
   - **Spin and parity** of intermediate states
   - **CP violation** through asymmetries
   - **Final state interactions**

**Key advantages**:
- Phase space density is uniform if $|\mathcal{M}_{fi}|^2$ is constant
- Deviations reveal decay dynamics
- Powerful for studying complex decay chains
