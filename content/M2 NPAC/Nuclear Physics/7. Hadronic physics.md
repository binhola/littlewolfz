## Motivation and introduction
### Motivation: Why study the Strong interaction
Modern physics describe 4 fundamental forces. Three of them are well-known:
- **Electromagnetism:** QED, it is the most precisely tested theory in physics
- **Weak**: predict nuclear decay, unified with with EM in electroweak theory
- **Gravity**: GR, governs the large-scale universe

This leaves the **Strong** interaction, QCD, 1970s. The least understood in its *everyday manifestations* and crucial to study.
#### The puzzle of strong force
- The core challenge is **confinement**. 
- Fundamental particles: quarks and gluons are never observed in isolation. Permanently confined within protons, neutrons and other hadrons.
- Hard to predict how quarks and gluons form the matter we see.

**Paradox**: the theory is simple, but its consequences are complex.
#### Why should we care?
1. **Origin of mass:**
	- Most mass of the visible universe does not come from Higgs boson.
	- Over 99% of mass of protons and neutrons come from the intense energy of the strong force binding the quarks together. *We do not fully understand how this mass emerges from the interactions of nearly massless quarks and gluons*.
2. **Structure of matter**:
	- What does a proton look like in terms of its quarks and gluons? How are its properties like spin and charge distributed?
	- Are there *exotic forms of matter* made of quarks and gluons that we haven't discovered yet?
	- How does the proton's structure change when it is inside a nucleus?
3. **Phases of nuclear matter**:
	- In the early universe, moments after the Big Bang, the universe is filled with a hot "soup" of free quarks and gluons called **Quark-Gluon Plasma** (QGP).
	- Recreate in particle colliders to study its properties and understand how it "froze" into protons and neutrons.
	- Help understand extreme astrophysical objects like neutron stars.
4. **Testing ground for fundamental physics**:
	- Study how complex systems emerge from simple fundamental laws.
	- Push the knowledge boundaries in thermodynamics, many-body physics, and the transition from microscopic to macroscopic world.
#### Key questions in strong interaction physics
- How do quarks and gluons build the protons and neutrons that we observe?
- Properties of exotic hadrons and QGP?
- How strong force generates most of the mass of ordinary matter?
- The nature of **confinement** and **hadronisation**?

### Introduction: scales and rules of the game
Natural units
$$
\hbar = c = k_B = 1
$$
Distance, time, and mass in unit of energy (eV). Two key conversion factors:
- $\hbar c = 197 \rm \, MeV \cdot fm$: connect energy/momentum scales to length scales.
- $k_B \cdot 293 K \approx 1/40 \rm \, eV$: connect temperature to energy

Typical scales in strong interaction:
- **Energy/momentum**: a few MeV to 100 GeV
- **Length**: 0.001 to 10 fm (A proton is 1 fm in diameter)

### Strong interaction in Standard Model
Strong interaction is described by **Quantum Chromodynamics** (QCD), theory of quarks and gluons.
#### QCD Lagragian
$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{QCD, classical}} = \underbrace{-\dfrac{1}{4} F^A_{\mu \nu} F^{A, \mu \nu}}_{\text{gluon field}} + \underbrace{\sum_{f} \bar q_{a,f} (i \not D - m_f)_{ab} q_{b,f}}_{\text{gluon-quark field interaction}}
$$
Break down the key components:
- **Quarks and flavors $f$** : the quark field $q_f$is defined by its flavor $f$. This index runs over 6 possible types of quarks: u, d, c, s, t, b. They are different by their mass $m_f$
- **Color charge** (a, b) : each quark field $q_f$is a *triplet* in *color space*. The indices a, b runs from 1 to 3 labeled red, green, blue. 
- **Gluons** ($A_\mu^A$) : the massless spin-1 gluon field. The index $A$, running from 1 to 8, represents the eight types of gluons, each carrying a specific combination of color and anti-color charge.
- **Covariant derivative ($D_\mu$)** : generates the interactions. Acting on quarks (in the fundamental, triplet representation): 
$$
(D_\mu)_{ab} = \partial_\mu \delta_{ab} + i g(t^C)_{ab} A^C_\mu
$$
where $(t^C)_{ab}$are the generators of SU(3) (Gell-Mann matrices). This matrix structure means that when a quark emits or absorbs a gluon, it can change its color (e.g. a red quark $q_r$can turn into a blue quark $q_b$by emitting a red anti-blue gluon $r \bar b$)
- **Gluon field strength** ($F^A_{\mu \nu}$) : 
$$
F^A_{\mu \nu} = \partial_{\mu} A_\nu^A - \partial_\nu A^A_\mu - \underbrace{g f^{ABC} A^B_\mu A^C_\nu}_{\text{non-Abelian}}
$$
#### Key difference from QED: Self-interaction
Compare the QCD field strength tensor to QED. The first two term is similar but QCD has the third extra term.
- In QED, photons are *neutral* and do not interact with each other.
- In QCD, gluons themselves carry *color charge* (they live in adjoint representation of SU(3)).  The third non-Abelian term means gluons can interact with each other, leading to triple or quartic gluon vertices. This self-interaction is the direct cause of asymptotic freedom.

### Two pillars of QCD
QCD is defined by two fundamental, empirically verified properties.
#### Confinement
**Observation:** Isolated quarks and gluons are never observed in nature.

**Confinement:** At low energies and temperatures, the force between colored particles are not dimished with distance. Instead, it remains constant or grow, making it impossible to pull apart. They are permanently **confined** within *color-neutral bound states* called **hadrons**. 
- Typical confinement scale is **1 fm**, a size of a proton
- Lattice QCD simulations strongly confirm confinement, no rigorous mathematical proof yet.

#### Asymptotic freedom
**Observation:** At very high energy (short distance), quarks and gluons behave as nearly free particles.

**Asymptotic freedom:** the strong coupling constant $\alpha_s = \dfrac{g^2}{4 \pi}$becomes weaker at high energies or large momentum transfer (Q).

The running coupling, to leading order:
$$
\alpha_s(Q^2) = \dfrac{1}{b \ln(Q^2/\Lambda_{\rm QCD})}
$$
where $b = \dfrac{1}{12 \pi}(33 - 2n_f)$and $n_f$ is the number of active quark flavors.

- **Asymptotic freedom** justifies the perturbation theory at high energies.
- **Unique**: QCD is the only known physical quantum field theory in four dimensions to have this property. The positive value of b (due to the gluon self-interaction) is essential.
- **It defines the theory.** The scale $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$​ (~200 MeV) is a fundamental parameter of QCD, setting the scale for all non-perturbative phenomena like confinement.
- **Experimental verification**: The running of $\alpha_s$has been measured and verified.
